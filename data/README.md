# Practice data: Line D (synthetic)

A fictional cereal line with seven zones, two years of maintenance history (**1 Jul 2024 – 30 Jun 2026**), and vibration data. **Nothing here is real company data.** It's generated by [`generate_data.py`](generate_data.py) with a fixed seed, so every run produces identical files and the lesson answers always match.

```powershell
python data\generate_data.py      # rebuilds everything below in ~2 seconds, standard library only
```

## Monthly export columns (`exports/work_orders/`)

`WO Number, Asset, Asset Description, Zone, Criticality, WO Type, PM Ref, Description, Failure Code, Raised, Due, Completed, Status, Labour Hrs, Downtime Mins, Technician`. These are the same work orders, flattened the way a CMMS export usually is. Dates are **UK format**, which is why Phase 1 teaches locale-aware typing. Files are split by the month the WO was **raised**.

## How the simulation works (so the patterns you find are real)

- Each asset type has **competing failure modes**, each with its own **Weibull** shape β and life η. Examples: flow wrapper jaws β = 3 (wear-out), jams β ≈ 1 (random), sensors β < 1, and new installs get an extra *Installation defect* mode with β = 0.5 (infant mortality).
- **PM effects:** *Replacement* PMs renew their target mode; *Lubrication* takes 40 % off the target mode's age; *Inspections* sometimes catch a wear-out mode near the end of its life, which creates a planned **Corrective** job instead of a breakdown.
- **PM compliance varies by zone:** Primary Packing (~66 %) and End of Line (~75 %) are deliberately weaker than the others (~82–88 %).
- **Deliberate stories to discover:** the flow wrapper jaw replacement interval (182 days) is far longer than the jaws' life (η ≈ 86 days), which is the Phase 5 study. Late PMs *look* linked to breakdowns until you control for asset type (Week 37). And packing minor stops are stable but large (Week 38).
- **Minor stops** are random short stops (2–15 min), concentrated in packing and end of line, with no work order.
- **Vibration data:** degradation starts at a random point late in each bearing's life (the P on the P-F curve), and RMS, kurtosis, crest factor and temperature then rise along a curve. Waveforms are a shaft tone plus noise, plus outer-race impacts at **BPFO = 3.585 × shaft speed (88.2 Hz)**, ringing a ~1.2 kHz resonance.
